// Understanding the Problem

A crisis accelerating faster than the law.

Generative AI has made it dramatically easier to create convincing, non-consensual explicit imagery — and South Korea has become the country most affected by it. The people most exposed are young, and most are women.

Why this keeps happening

01

No built-in safeguards

Generative AI reached the public before any real filtering system was built in — so the same tools used for legitimate work can be used for abuse.

02

Platforms reward attention

Content spreads faster than moderation can review it, and engagement-driven platforms end up amplifying harmful material before it's caught.

03

Law hasn't caught up

Existing statutes assume a clear perpetrator in a clear jurisdiction. Deepfake abuse crosses borders and hides behind anonymity.

// Our Approach

Three initiatives, working together.

We don't treat victim support and policy change as separate tracks — the cases we hear directly shape the reforms we push for.

  1. 01 — ADVOCATE

    Push for stronger platform protocols: a direct line to Seoul law enforcement and the platforms themselves, with regular, mandatory checks on flagged content instead of relying on victims to report after the fact.

  2. 02 — SUPPORT

    Build a free victim support hub connecting people affected by deepfake abuse with legal guidance and confidential emotional support.

  3. 03 — DETECT

    Develop recommendations for platform-level safeguards — watermarking standards, faster takedown pathways — using patterns from real reports to show regulators where current systems fall short.

// Where We're Headed

Our goals

Short term (1–2 years)

  • Raise baseline trust and technical confidence around social media safety, tracked through embedded surveys.
  • Open formal communication with Seoul law enforcement and legislators on platform policy.
  • Launch an accessible victim support hub for reporting and guided next steps.
  • Publish regular reports summarizing what we're hearing from victims.

Long term (3–5 years)

  • Measurable reduction in deepfake content reaching young people on major platforms in Seoul.
  • Expand our victim support network beyond South Korea to other parts of East Asia.
  • Increase the share of victims willing to come forward and report abuse.
  • Push major platforms toward regular public transparency reporting.

// If You Need Help Right Now

Real support exists — today.

AISPA is a growing initiative, but you don't have to wait for us to get help. South Korea already runs a national support system for digital sex crime victims.

National digital sex crime support hotline — 1366

South Korea's Ministry of Gender Equality and Family operates the Digital Sex Crime Victim Support Center, providing counseling, content removal help, and evidence support 24 hours a day. Regional centers are being unified under a single hotline: 1366.

Source: Korea.net, official government news service. This is an independent, existing government resource — not run by AISPA.
